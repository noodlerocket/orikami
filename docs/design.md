# ORIKAMI — Game Design Document

Nov 20, 2026 · @Julian Uribe

## Concept

*Orikami* (折神, "folded god") is a 2D samurai metroidvania set in a world folded from a single sacred sheet of paper. The title is a pun: in Japanese, *kami* means both **paper** (紙) and **god** (神). Here the gods are paper and paper is sacred, and the whole game grows out of that.

**Logline:** A blank, nameless paper samurai climbs out of a well to save a land that is bleeding its own memory away. Then he learns that the Rain started the moment he was folded.

**Pillars**

- **Paper is identity.** Creases are memory and ink is history. Losing your shape means forgetting who you are.
- **The blade of a samurai.** Combat is built on the parry and the iaijutsu quick-draw. It should feel more like *Sekiro* than a nail-swinging bug.
- **Your body tells the story.** The Blank Ronin is slowly inked by everything he absorbs, and that ink decides which endings are open to him.
- **Light through paper.** Backlit shoji, glowing lanterns and shadow-puppet layers are the heart of the visual identity.

**Inspirations:** *Hollow Knight* (structure, tone, wordless protagonist), *Sekiro* (parry-driven combat), Japanese folklore (tsukumogami, senbazuru) and origami, kirigami and shadow-puppet theater. The goal is to borrow the genre's conventions while keeping the story's beats its own.

## The World: Kamiyo, the Folded Land

*Kamiyo* is a real word for the age of the gods, and here it also means "the paper world."

In the beginning there was one perfect, uncut sheet. The **First Folder**, a god whose face is never shown, folded it into mountains, rivers, people and the **Paper Emperor**, who ruled from a castle at the top of the world.

### The Wearing Sheet

Every fold weakens paper. After untold ages of folding, the world's oldest creases have begun to crack. The mountains split along their folds and the rivers fray at their edges. The First Folder knows that if nothing changes, Kamiyo will tear apart and be lost entirely.

There is only one cure. The sheet must be **unfolded, smoothed and folded again**, so that the paper can rest and be made new. The Folder isn't destroying the world out of boredom or wrath. It is trying to save the paper itself. But an unfolded world remembers nothing, so everyone in Kamiyo would forget who they were.

The Folder also carries shame. Before Kamiyo, it crumpled and threw away thousands of failed attempts at creation. It doesn't want this world to end up on that heap. The rejected sheets still exist. They are piled in a forgotten place, and they became the Gashadokuro.

### The Rain

A hundred years ago, the **Rain** began.

It isn't ordinary rain. It's a slow, creeping damp that makes ink **bleed**. When paper gets wet, its creases soften. When a folded being loses its creases, it forgets its shape, and with its shape it forgets *who it was*. The sodden ones wander the land as limp, smeared husks that attack anything still crisp. Whole provinces now lie soggy and silent, and the ink of their histories has run into black pools.

Nobody in Kamiyo knows where the Rain comes from. Most believe it is a curse.

## Protagonist: The Blank Ronin

You are a samurai folded from **unmarked paper**: no ink, no family crest, no name. Everyone else in Kamiyo is printed with patterns, words and heraldry. You are pure white. A *ronin* is a masterless samurai, and "blank" fits that twice over: you have no master and no name.

You wake at the bottom of a well in a ruined village, with a katana made from a single razor-sharp fold sheathed at your hip. You never speak.

### What he looks like

The Ronin's design is fixed by the walking figure in the rainy-town reference picture. He is a slender traveller folded entirely from white washi, seen side-on:

- **Hat (kasa):** a wide, shallow paper cone, about half as wide as he is tall, tipped slightly forward so the brim hides his eyes. It is the same white paper as the rest of him, not straw-coloured. The hat is his silhouette.
- **Face:** only the lower face shows under the brim, in shadow. It is a flat, blank mask with a narrow chin and two slanted eye slits. No mouth.
- **Cowl:** paper gathered in thick folds round the neck. White, like everything else. There is no colour on him at all until ink marks him.
- **Robe and cloak:** a long robe to mid-calf with a ragged hem, a sash at the waist, a short mantle over the shoulders and wide sleeves. From his back hangs a cloak torn into **long pointed tails**. They hang down when he stands and stream out behind him when he moves.
- **Legs:** slim, with small folded feet, striding out from under the robe.
- **Katana:** sheathed at the left hip, the handle pointing forward: black wrap with small pale diamonds and a dark guard. One hand rests on the handle. The blade is only out while he strikes: every slash is a quick draw, and it is back in the scabbard a moment later.

Every surface is a flat fold with its own shade, and the paper shows a fine crumple. In motion he is a rigid paper puppet: the pieces never bend, they only hinge.

(The origami samurai sheet with the horned helmet is kept as a reference for folding style and faceting only, not for his design.)

### Why the Rain can't touch you

**The Rain can't bleed what was never inked.** It erases identity, and you have none to erase. That makes you the only being in Kamiyo who can't be corrupted.

You are still paper, though. Water still **weighs you down**: in heavy rain you sag, jump lower and move slower. Deep ink pools stain you quickly. The Rain can't take your memory, but it can make you heavy.

### The slowly inking body (core mechanic)

You begin blank, and you don't stay that way. Every time you strike an enemy, you absorb a little of their spilled ink. That ink powers healing and techniques, and it also **marks you permanently**.

- Over the game, faint patterns, crests and fragments of words appear on your white body. They come from the people and creatures you fought.
- The marks are cosmetic at first. Later they start to matter: NPCs react to them, some doors only open for a marked Ronin, and some charms only work on a blank one.
- How much ink you carry by the end decides which endings are open to you (see Endings). A heavily inked Ronin can take on the Emperor's grief. A Ronin who stayed close to blank can follow the Folder.
- At certain shrines you can **wash** ink away, at a cost. This lets players steer toward the ending they want.

This is what makes the protagonist different from Hollow Knight's Knight. The Knight is empty and stays empty. The Ronin starts empty and is slowly written on by the world.

## Key Characters

### Akane, the Vermilion Fox

A fox-masked swordswoman (*akane* means "madder red") who duels you several times across the game. She is the Emperor's sworn guard and the only person who knows why he wept. At first she tries to stop you from reaching the castle, because she believes you were sent to end the world. She's right.

Over time she becomes an uneasy ally. Her vermilion mask is the brightest color in the game, and in a land of bleeding ink her red never runs. Late in the story you learn why: she is folded from the one sheet of the Emperor's own paper he cut from his sleeve.

### The Three Generals

The castle is bound by three great **hanko (seal stamps)**, one held by each of the Emperor's generals. Each general fled to a distant province and is dealing with the Rain in a different way.

| General | Province | How they face the Rain | The fight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Shimeri, the Sodden General** | Sumi Marsh | Endures it. He is soaked through, barely holding his shape, and still guarding his post out of duty. | Slow and heavy. He fights with fading honor and forgets his own moves mid-fight. |
| **Hōshō, the Gilded General** | The Lantern Market | Hides from it. She covered herself in gold leaf so water can't reach her, and now she can't feel anything either. | Fast and flashy. Gold flakes peel off as she's damaged and reveal blank paper underneath. |
| **Tsugi, the Mended General** | Yukiyama Peaks | Repairs himself endlessly. He is patched with scraps from fallen soldiers until little of the original is left. | He uses the techniques of everyone he's patched with. The question is whether he is still himself. |

You can **defeat** each general and take the seal, or **free** them by finding what they lost. Freed generals appear in the endings.

### Others

- **The Paper-Maker** (Kamiyama Village): An old woman who pulls you from the well. She makes fresh sheets by hand, and she is the first to notice your marks.
- **Hanshi** (cartographer): Named after a calligraphy paper. She sells hand-painted scroll maps that unroll as you explore.
- **The Tanuki Merchant:** Runs a stall that appears in odd places and takes Mon. He always seems to know more than he admits.
- **The Paper Emperor:** Unseen until the end. His voice echoes through the castle as rain.

## Story Arc

The central twist: **the Ronin isn't the cure for the Rain. He is its cause.**

1. **Awakening.** You climb out of the well into Kamiyama Village, a hamlet half-crumpled by the Rain. The Paper-Maker tells you the Emperor has sealed himself inside his castle and the land is dying. Your blankness makes you the land's only hope, or so everyone believes.
2. **The Three Seals.** To open the castle you must take the three hanko from Shimeri, Hōshō and Tsugi, by defeating them or freeing them. Along the way Akane hunts you and warns you that you don't know what you are.
3. **The Revelation.** In the Burnt Archive you piece together the truth from half-burned scrolls. The Rain is not a curse. **It is the Emperor's tears.** A hundred years ago he learned that the First Folder means to unfold the world, and he has wept ever since. His grief soaked the land.
4. **The Origin.** The final scrolls explain what you are. A hundred years ago, the First Folder made **the first crease of the new world**: a figure folded from the blank reverse side of the sheet, then left to lie dormant at the bottom of a well. That crease was you. The moment you were folded, the unfolding began, and the Emperor felt it and began to weep. You didn't arrive to stop the Rain. **The Rain is grief for what your existence will do.**
5. **The Castle.** You climb Shiro, the Paper Castle, knowing that the Emperor sees you as the end of everything. What you do at the top depends on the ink you carry and the choices you made along the way.

**Foreshadowing to plant early:** the Rain is heaviest around Kamiyama's well. The Paper-Maker says the old creases in the village have been relaxing for a hundred years. Akane refuses to fight you near the well. The Gashadokuro, made of the Folder's rejected sheets, recognizes you as one of its own.

## Endings

The ending you reach depends on two things: **how much ink your body carries** when you reach the Emperor, and whether you found **all 1,000 paper cranes**.

| Ending | Requirement | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Refold** (sacrifice) | Heavily inked Ronin | Your body is already half-written with the land's memories, so you can hold the rest. You draw the Rain into yourself and become fully inked for the first time. The Emperor's grief becomes yours, and you stand forever as Kamiyo's guardian. The sheet keeps wearing out, but slowly, and the world keeps its memory. It's saved, but on borrowed time. |
| **Unfold** (release) | Ronin who stayed close to blank | You finish what you were folded to do. In the final cutscene Kamiyo gently unfolds into a single white sheet. The creases rest, the paper heals, and a new crane begins to fold. Nobody remembers, but the world goes on. It's bittersweet and hopeful. |
| **The Thousand Cranes** (true ending) | All 1,000 cranes found, plus at least two generals freed | Legend says folding 1,000 cranes grants a wish (*senbazuru*). You wish for a third path. The Folder doesn't have to unfold the world, because the people of Kamiyo can refold themselves: each crane is a fresh crease, freely given. The Emperor and the Folder reconcile, and the Rain becomes ordinary rain. Flowers grow from paper for the first time. |

The Refold ending deliberately echoes the protagonist's arc: a blank figure who is filled in by everyone he met. The Unfold ending lets the player accept the Folder's reasoning, which is sympathetic because the sheet really is wearing out.

## Gameplay

### Combat: Built on the Parry

- **Parry:** The center of combat. Perfectly timed blocks deflect attacks, build enemy **posture** damage and stagger them. A parried enemy creases, and a staggered one is open for a finishing cut. This is where Orikami should feel most unlike Hollow Knight.
- **Slash:** Fast, crisp katana strikes in four directions, including a downward strike for pogo bouncing.
- **Iaijutsu Draw:** Hold attack to sheathe, then release for a lightning quick-draw dash-slash. It's high risk and high reward, and a parry followed straight into a draw is the signature combo.
- **Ink:** Hitting enemies absorbs their spilled ink. Spend it to **Refold**, smoothing your creases to heal, or on special techniques. Every drop you absorb also marks your body permanently (see Protagonist).

### Movement Abilities (Metroidvania Gates)

Each ability is an origami fold you learn.

| Ability | Fold | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Crane Glide** | Tsuru | Unfold paper wings to glide across gaps |
| **Shuriken Dash** | Shuriken | A spinning air-dash that cuts through thin paper walls |
| **Flat Fold** | Hira-ori | Fold yourself paper-thin to slip through cracks and under doors |
| **Fan Gust** | Uchiwa | Double jump by flapping a fan beneath you |
| **Wax Coat** | Rōbiki | Repel water so the Rain no longer weighs you down, and wade through ink pools without being stained |
| **Shadow Step** | Kage-e | Step *behind* glowing shoji screens to move through a silhouette layer of the world |

**Shadow Step** is the signature mechanic. Some areas have a hidden backlit layer where you exist only as a black silhouette. It's shadow-puppet theater turned into platforming. One more idea: in the silhouette layer your ink marks don't show, so a heavily inked Ronin can pass guards that only let blank ones through.

### Other Systems

- **Shrines (rest points):** Small roadside *hokora* where you rest, save and equip charms. A lantern lights when you arrive. Some shrines can also wash ink from your body, at a cost.
- **Omamori (charms):** Equip protective temple amulets for perks, such as extra ink gain, a longer blade or faster iaijutsu. Some only work on a blank Ronin and some only on a heavily inked one.
- **Mon (currency):** Old coins you collect and spend at the Tanuki Merchant's stall.
- **Death:** When you die you leave behind a **Smudge**: a smeared ink copy of yourself, wearing your marks and holding your lost Mon. Return and cut through it to reclaim them.
- **Maps:** Hanshi sells hand-painted scroll maps that unroll as you explore.
- **Paper cranes:** 1,000 hidden cranes. They're found in bundles, not one at a time, so collecting them doesn't become a grind.

Each region gets one dominant tone, with vermilion reserved for things that matter.

**Every region must feel alive** (Julian): things sway, give under the Ronin's weight, splash, scatter and react as he passes. Movement has quality-of-life care in the Hollow Knight tradition: forgiving jumps, clear tells, quick returns to the last shrine.

## Bosses: Paper Yōkai

Japanese folklore has **tsukumogami**, objects that come alive after 100 years. The Rain has fallen for exactly 100 years, so the abandoned paper things of Kamiyo are waking up all at once.

- **Kasa-obake:** A hopping one-eyed paper umbrella that spins and rains needles. It's the first boss and teaches parry timing.
- **Chōchin-obake:** A haunted lantern that sets the arena on fire. Paper vs. fire is deadly.
- **Ittan-momen:** A long flying strip that tries to wrap and strangle you, in an aerial fight.
- **Jorōgumo:** A spider-woman who weaves the arena out of red string.
- **Gashadokuro:** A colossal skeleton made of thousands of crumpled, rejected sheets, the First Folder's failed attempts at creation. It recognizes you as another of the Folder's makings and asks, without words, why you got to be kept.
- **The Three Generals:** Shimeri, Hōshō and Tsugi (see Key Characters). Each can be defeated or freed.
- **Akane, the Vermilion Fox** (recurring rival): Duels you several times. Each fight is harder, and the last one is a test she wants you to pass.
- **The Weeping Emperor** (final): A two-phase fight. Phase two takes place in a flooding throne room as the Emperor's grief overwhelms everything. Your ink level changes the fight: a heavily inked Ronin is slowed by the water, but can absorb the Emperor's tears to heal.

## Art and Sound Direction

**Palette:** Off-white *washi* paper, deep *sumi* ink black, indigo, with **vermilion red** as the accent color. Red marks the important things: torii gates, blood-like ink and Akane's mask.

**Look and feel**

- **Layered diorama depth:** Each background is cut-paper layers with soft drop shadows between them, like a shadow box or *kirigami* scene. Parallax makes it feel physical.
- **Backlighting:** Lanterns glow *through* the paper, so light comes from behind surfaces like a shoji screen. This one effect will carry most of the beauty.
- **Paper texture:** A subtle fiber grain over everything, slightly rough cut edges and visible fold lines on characters.
- **The rainy town look (Kamiyama Village):** Fixed by the rainy-town reference picture. A muted indigo night, never a bright blue. Every sheet has a **torn edge with a thin pale rim** where the paper's core shows. Each layer further back is paler and bluer, with a sheet of mist at its foot. Houses are dark boards with peeling pale paper, ink running down it in drips, and roofs of small overlapping shingles with a wet pale lip. Warm amber comes only from lanterns and shoji windows, which glow, light the walls near them and smear across the wet paving. The view is framed by near-black foreground shapes (grass, rocks) and a cherry branch in blossom. Vermilion is kept for the torii, a banner and the lantern crests.
- **The Ronin's look:** See "What he looks like" under the protagonist: wide white kasa hat, long cloak with torn tails, sheathed katana, all white.
- **The inking Ronin:** The Ronin's sprite gains ink patterns as the game goes on, from faint traces to dense calligraphy. Players should be able to tell how far along someone is by a screenshot.
- **Stop-motion animation:** Animate at about 12fps ("on twos") so movement feels handmade, like puppetry.
- **Wind:** The wind that drives the Rain is felt everywhere. Lanterns swing, shop curtains and banners flap, grass leans, and the Ronin's cloak streams out behind him. Gusts roll along the street, so you see one coming.
- **Decay, not spring:** Cherry trees are half bare, their blossom faded and dusty. Nothing in Kamiyo is in bloom.
- **Being struck:** When the Ronin is hurt, ink splashes off him and soaks his white paper black; the blots then shrink away. He never flickers in and out.
- **Satisfying feedback:** Enemies tear into confetti scraps when they die. When you die, your figure unfolds into a flat sheet and flutters to the ground. Damaged enemies get crumpled.

**Sound:** Shakuhachi flute and koto for exploration, taiko drums for bosses, rustling paper footsteps, and sword hits that sound like a crisp paper cut. Rain should always be audible somewhere in the mix until the true ending, where it's gone for the first time.

## Production Notes: Godot and GitHub Pages

- **Use Godot 4 with the Compatibility renderer.** It's the most reliable choice for web exports.
- **Export single-threaded.** GitHub Pages can't set the special server headers that multithreaded web builds need. Godot 4.3 and later let you turn off thread support in the web export settings, and then the build works on GitHub Pages without extra tricks.
- **Useful Godot features:** `Parallax2D` for the layered backgrounds, `PointLight2D` for lantern glow, and a simple shader for the paper grain and ink-bleed effects. The same shader can drive the Ronin's ink marks with a mask texture that fills in as ink is absorbed.
