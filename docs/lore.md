# ORIKAMI — The Lore, in Order

Everything that happens in Kamiyo, from before the world to the endings, in
the order it happened. The game is built in this same order (see "The game,
chapter by chapter" at the end).

`docs/design.md` is the source for the big picture. Parts marked
**(proposed)** are new here, drafts for Julian to keep, change or throw out.

---

## 1. Before the world

There was one sheet. Uncut, uncreased, perfectly white on both sides. Nothing
was written on it, so it remembered nothing and lacked nothing.

The **First Folder** is the god who picked it up. Its face is never shown;
when it appears at all it is a pair of hands, or the shadow of hands on a
screen.

The Folder did not get it right the first time. Before Kamiyo it folded and
crumpled and threw away thousands of failed worlds. They were never
destroyed, only thrown aside, and they lie in a forgotten place: the
**Crumple Heap**. Much later they will rise together as the Gashadokuro.

## 2. The Folding of Kamiyo

At last the Folder made one world it kept. Each fold became something:
- the hard folds became **mountains**, the soft ones **rivers**;
- small, careful folds became **people**, animals and spirits;
- one great fold at the top of the sheet became the **Paper Emperor**, and
  around him **Shiro, the Paper Castle**, at the top of the world.

The front of the sheet was printed: every being in Kamiyo carries patterns,
words, family crests. **Creases are memory and ink is history.** A person is
their folds: lose the folds and you lose who you were. The back of the sheet,
the side no one sees, was left blank.

The world was called **Kamiyo**, "the age of the gods", which here also means
"the paper world", because in this land *kami* (god) and *kami* (paper) are
the same word.

## 3. The Age of Creases

For untold ages Kamiyo was crisp. The Emperor ruled from Shiro. His three
generals, **Shimeri**, **Hōshō** and **Tsugi**, each held one of his **hanko**,
the seal stamps that bind the castle's gate. Villages grew in the folds of
the valleys. One of them was **Kamiyama**, below the castle, built round a
deep stone well, with a shrine on the hill at its far end.

At the Kamiyama shrine there was a dance each spring, danced in a **hannya
mask** (a noh mask of a woman whose grief turned her into a demon). The mask
was very old. **(proposed)**

## 4. The Wearing

Every fold weakens paper. After ages of folding, the oldest creases began to
crack: mountains split along their folds, rivers frayed at their edges.

The Folder saw that Kamiyo would tear apart. There was one cure: **unfold
the sheet, smooth it, and fold it again**, so the paper could rest and be made
new. But an unfolded world remembers nothing. Everyone in Kamiyo would forget
who they were.

The Folder chose to do it anyway, out of love for the paper, and out of shame
at the Crumple Heap: it would not let this world end up on that pile.

## 5. One hundred years ago: the First Crease

The Folder began by making **the first crease of the new world**. It turned
the sheet over to the blank side no one had ever seen and folded one figure
from it: a samurai, with no ink, no crest and no name, and a katana made from
a single razor-sharp fold. It laid him at the bottom of the **well in
Kamiyama**, to wait.

That was **the Blank Ronin**. The moment he was folded, the unfolding began.

At the top of the world, the Emperor felt that crease and understood what it
meant. He wept. **His tears are the Rain**, and they have not stopped since.

That same night:
- The Emperor sealed himself inside Shiro and gave each general one hanko to
  carry far away, so the gate could not be opened easily.
- He cut one piece from his own sleeve and folded **Akane, the Vermilion
  Fox**, as his guard. Because she is the Emperor's own paper, her red never
  runs in the Rain. She is the only one who knows why he weeps.

## 6. The hundred years of Rain

The Rain is not ordinary rain. It makes ink **bleed**. Wet paper loses its
creases, and a being that loses its creases forgets its shape and who it was.
The soaked ones wander as limp, smeared **husks** that attack anything still
crisp. Whole provinces have gone soggy and silent; their histories have run
into black pools. No one knows where the Rain comes from. Most call it a
curse.

The Rain is **heaviest around Kamiyama's well**, though nobody knows why.

**The generals**, far away, each faced it in their own way: Shimeri endures
it in Sumi Marsh, soaked and barely holding his shape. Hōshō hides from it
under gold leaf in the Lantern Market and can no longer feel anything. Tsugi
in Yukiyama keeps patching himself with the paper of fallen soldiers until
little of him is left.

**Tsukumogami.** In Japanese folklore, things that reach a hundred years old
wake up. The Rain has fallen for exactly a hundred years, so the abandoned
paper things of Kamiyo are now waking, all at once.

### The Weeping Hannya **(proposed)**

The last dancer of the Kamiyama shrine was a woman called **Shizu**. When the
Rain began, the village asked her to dance the mask's dance to plead for it
to stop. She danced it every night, for years, in the shrine hall by lamp
light. The Rain did not stop.

The mask was old enough to wake. What it woke to was grief: the Emperor's
tears, falling on the shrine roof night after night, soaking through the
paper of Shizu's face. Slowly she forgot her own face, and the mask
remembered one for her. In the end the mask was her face: *she who wept
until her face became a mask*.

Now she kneels behind the shoji of her shrine hall, weeping with the
Emperor's grief, and she guards the shrine courtyard: the only way out of
Kamiyama toward the Bamboo of Folded Wind. She weeps hardest when the Ronin
comes near. Like the Emperor, she can feel what he is.

## 7. The game, in story order

### Prologue: the title
The title screen shows the Ronin standing on a cliff above the valley,
looking at Kamiyama, the river and its red bridge, and Shiro on its crag
beyond, under a red sun. It is the whole journey in one picture: where he
starts, and where he must go.

### Chapter 1: Awakening (Kamiyama Village)
1. **The well.** He wakes at the bottom of the well, blank, in the dark,
   with rain falling on him from the circle of sky above. He climbs out.
2. **The Paper-Maker.** An old woman who makes fresh sheets by hand finds
   him. She tells him (in pictures, as he never speaks, and she barely does)
   that the Emperor has shut himself away, the land is dying, and his
   blankness makes him the only one the Rain cannot unmake. She hints that
   the village's old creases have been relaxing for a hundred years. She is
   the first to notice when ink starts to mark him.
3. **The village.** Husks in the street. He learns to cut, parry, draw and
   heal, climbs the gatehouse walls, and reaches the torii.
4. **The Weeping Hannya.** Her shadow on the shoji, the paper rips, the
   fight.
5. **After her (proposed).** Her mask cracks and falls. For a moment Shizu's
   own face shows, washed blank, and she is at peace. The lanterns relight,
   the Rain eases for a breath, and the far gate of the courtyard opens onto
   the road to the bamboo. On the shoji, the last thing her shadow shows is
   a figure climbing out of a well: the first hint of what he is.

### Chapter 2: The Bamboo of Folded Wind
Towering green paper strips that sway, calm, airy and dangerous. He learns
the **Crane Glide** (Tsuru): unfolding paper wings to cross gaps. Akane
appears for the first time and warns him that he does not know what he is.
**(proposed: Akane's first duel ends this chapter.)**

### The middle of the game (open world)
From here the land opens up, metroidvania style, and can be explored in many
orders: the **Lantern Market** (Hōshō), **Sumi Marsh** (Shimeri), **Koinobori
Skies**, the **Thousand Gates**, **Yukiyama Peaks** (Tsugi). Each general can
be beaten for their seal or freed by finding what they lost. More abilities
(Shuriken Dash, Flat Fold, Fan Gust, Wax Coat, Shadow Step) open new paths.
Akane duels him again, harder each time. The **Crumple Heap** is hidden
somewhere; there the Gashadokuro asks him, without words, why he was the one
the Folder kept.

### The Revelation: the Burnt Archive
In a library of half-burned scrolls he pieces together the truth: the Rain is
the Emperor's tears, and the Emperor weeps because of him. He was the first
crease of the unfolding.

### The Castle
With the three seals he climbs Shiro. The higher he climbs, the louder the
weeping. The Weeping Emperor fights in two phases; the second in a throne
room flooding with tears.

### The endings
Decided by how much ink his body carries, and whether he found all 1,000
paper cranes:
- **Refold** (heavily inked): he draws the Rain into himself and stands
  forever as Kamiyo's guardian. The world keeps its memory, on borrowed time.
- **Unfold** (near blank): Kamiyo gently unfolds into one white sheet, and a
  new crane begins to fold. Nobody remembers; the world goes on.
- **The Thousand Cranes** (all cranes, two or more generals freed): a wish
  for a third path. The people refold themselves, crease by crease, freely
  given. The Rain becomes ordinary rain. Flowers grow from paper for the
  first time.
